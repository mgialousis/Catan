## Follow-up: your own roll never animated

Two defects the user reported after playing build 18, both in the roll
presentation and both fixed client-side.

**Your own roll showed no dice and no resource flights, while a bot's roll
animated normally.** The board was an unkeyed child of the panel list. Sending a
command inserts a "sending" notice above it and the acknowledgement removes it
again, so the board's index shifted twice on every command a person issued.
Unkeyed list children are matched by position, so each shift destroyed and
rebuilt the board's element: `didUpdateWidget` never saw the roll transition,
and the presentation state — and the camera transform with it — was thrown away.
A bot's roll sets no pending notice, which is why only your own roll was
affected. The board now carries a stable key.

Keying it surfaced a latent crash: the overlay measures the board through
`localToGlobal` in the same frame the list relays out, when the sliver child has
no layout offset yet and the transform walk throws. The measurement now reports
"not yet measurable" and skips that frame.

**The cyan production rings sometimes disagreed with the dice on screen.** The
rings were read from the live snapshot while the overlay presented an older,
queued roll, and ending a turn clears the dice, which emptied the rings from
under a presentation still running. Rolls are no longer queued at all — a newer
roll takes over, since a backlog arrives too late to describe the table and the
first board touch discarded it anyway — and the rings are now driven by the roll
being presented rather than by whatever the snapshot currently holds.

Validation: 345 Flutter tests pass (three added) with clean analysis. The new
tests reproduce both defects first — the own-roll one end to end through
`GameScreen` with a real `ROLL_DICE` command and its pending notice — and the
test that previously asserted the queue now asserts that a superseded roll is
dropped rather than replayed late.

## Presentation ordering, map layout and piece feedback

Four commits the same day, driven by watching a practice game: `5d3ecd6`,
`b547eaf`, `9f5a08b`, `fe0fc39`.

### Presentations play in order

Roll, payout and the next roll overlapped. The client now keeps a short ordered
queue and plays one presentation at a time; your own roll still takes over,
because it is what you are waiting on before you can act. A roll hands the board
back centred rather than to wherever the camera started. Another seat placing a
piece is presented too: the camera eases to it, holds, names who built what and
recentres, with the location derived from diffing consecutive snapshots rather
than from a wire change. The close-up stops at 1.38x, below the terrain
resolution bucket in `board.dart`, because crossing it rebakes the tiles and
would stutter the movement being watched.

Bot pacing now derives from the previous move rather than a flat 900 ms: a roll
is left alone for the length of its payout, a build for its close-up. This is
what makes the ordering real rather than merely buffered, at the cost of
practice games running slower.

**A bug in that pacing, found by checking rather than assuming.** `botPace`
counted the payout by reading `action` on the stored activity entries, but
`projectEffects` renames the engine's `action` to `type` before the server
persists it. Every roll therefore counted zero cards and got the dice-only
2.8 s pause, so builds still landed mid-payout — exactly what the pacing was
added to prevent. The unit test asserted the same wrong shape, which is why it
passed; it now builds its fixtures by calling `projectEffects` itself. Measured
in the live practice game after `9f5a08b` deployed:

| Previous move | Cards paid | Gap to next move |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| ROLL_DICE | 5 | 13.3 s |
| ROLL_DICE | 5 | 10.6 s |
| ROLL_DICE | 4 | 9.4 s |
| ROLL_DICE | 0 | 3.4 s |
| BUILD_ROAD | — | 3.2 s |
| END_TURN | — | 1.2 s |

Before the fix, a roll paying 4–6 cards was followed by a build after 3.4 s.

### The map owns the screen

The map was a strip: 358x323 of an 844-tall phone in portrait, with the four
seat badges taking 196 px above it; in landscape it was squeezed into the left
half with the panels beside it. Everything now reads top to bottom in both
orientations, the map runs edge to edge and each panel insets itself. On a short
landscape screen the map is fitted to the height, because sizing it to the width
put two thirds of it below the fold. The seats moved into the four corners of
the water, carrying points and resource cards, with development cards from
520 px and knights from 700 px. Below 340 px the row above the map is kept.

The turn, phase and roll moved onto the board's own header line beside the view
controls, removing another panel from above the map. Measured portrait result:
the map went from 358x323 at y=359 to 390x352 at y=211.

The key that keeps the board's element alive across sibling churn had to move to
the outermost list child once the board was wrapped: a key on an inner widget
does not save a subtree whose parent element is discarded.

### New pieces blink

A newly placed road, settlement or city pulses twice where it stands. Your own
placements blink without moving the camera or raising a banner. Reduced motion
marks the piece steadily rather than flashing.
